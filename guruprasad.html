Page 3 — The Greatness of Effort (Purusharth) — Swami Harihar Ji Maharaj

Effort is praised everywhere. Difficult tasks have been seen to succeed through diligence. A farmer goes to the field and receives a harvest according to the seed he sowed. Even destiny (prarabdha) does not work without effort. Good deeds bring happiness and bad deeds bring sorrow. Through austerity a person can attain good fortune and even the supreme goal. Those who do not make effort do not attain wealth, prosperity and success. When a person makes effort, he receives results in accordance with fate (daiva).

The Vedic saying is: "Na rite shrantasya sakhyaya devah" — the gods do not help a person until he has labored and become tired. Just as a single spark, aided by the wind, blazes into a great fire, so fate too becomes great with the help of effort. God, too, is the giver of fruit only to effort. As it is said:

"Yadyavat yadrisham yena kritam karma shubhashubham, tattavatra tadrisham tasya phalamishah prayachchhati."
— Whoever has performed whatever action, of whatever kind and measure, God gives him the fruit accordingly.

The maxim says:
"Udyamena hi sidhyanti karyani na manorathaih, nahi suptasya simhasya pravishanti mukhe mrigah."
— Tasks are accomplished by effort, not by wishes; deer do not walk into the mouth of a sleeping lion.

To bring change in the world's atmosphere, and to adorn human society with divine qualities, great effort is needed today. Geeta students should keep striving constantly in this direction.

— Swami Harihar Ji Maharaj

Page 5 — Divya Prasad: Divine Prasad of Brahmlin Swami Shri 1008 Harihar Ji Maharaj

Meaning and explanation of Shrimad Bhagavad Gita 2:20

Verse:
"Na jayate mriyate va kadachin nayam bhutva bhavita va na bhuyah;
Ajo nityah shashvato'yam purano na hanyate hanyamane sharire."

Meaning: This soul is never born and never dies; nor, having come into being, will it cease to be again. It is unborn, eternal, permanent and ancient. It is not killed when the body is killed.

Explanation: From verse 11 of Chapter 2, Lord Krishna has been continuously explaining to Arjuna the difference between body and soul. In this verse He brings in "time" (kala). The verse is divided into four parts:

  1. Na jayate mriyate va kadachit — The soul at no time is born, nor does it die. Death belongs only to what is born. The body is perishable, the embodied (soul) is imperishable — this was stated in verse 17 of this chapter. Here, through time, the difference is clarified. The Mahabharata (Shanti Parva) says: no one dies before time, nor is anyone born before time; even a small child does not begin to speak before its time. All beings on earth die only after growing ripe with time.

The scriptures list six modifications of the gross body: it exists, is born, grows, changes, decays and perishes — coming into the womb, birth, growth, procreation, decline and death. Because it is unchanging, the soul does not undergo these changes.

Krishna also says: "Mamaivamsho jivaloke jivabhutah sanatanah" (15:7) — the souls of this world are My eternal fragments. "Aham atma gudakesha sarvabhutashayasthitah; aham adish cha madhyam cha bhutanam anta eva cha" (10:20) — I am the Self seated in the heart of all beings; I am the beginning, middle and end of all beings. The Bhagavatam (5.18.13) says Hari is directly the Self of all embodied beings. The Katha Upanishad (1.2.18) and Brihadaranyaka Upanishad (4.4.25) say the same: the Self is blissful, unborn, imperishable, free from old age, deathless and fearless.

Waves arise and vanish, but the ocean neither arises nor vanishes; that which has a beginning has an end.

  1. Na ayam bhutva bhavita va na bhuyah — Not having come into being, it will not again cease to be (it does not arise and then exist for a time). The body, having come into being, is gone after some time; the soul never ceases to be.

  2. Ajo nityah shashvato'yam purano — Birthless, ever-existing, eternal and beginningless. Krishna gives four qualities of the soul: (a) unborn, (b) always the same, (c) eternal, (d) "purana" — ancient yet always fresh; never old, wasted or weak. All of these are the opposite for the body, which is born, changes every moment, ends in death, and is sometimes fat, sometimes weak, healthy, diseased, aged.

  3. Na hanyate hanyamane sharire — It is not destroyed when the body is destroyed. The body with its modifications is perishing every moment; the soul, being unborn, unchanging, eternal, is never destroyed.

Nowhere else in the Gita is the difference between soul and body shown so clearly as in these four phrases.

Jai Shri Krishna, Jai Gurudev

Page 8 — Special for Shri Hanuman Jayanti

"Jai Hanuman Gyan Gun Sagar" (Based on the divine discourses of Param Pujya Shri Sadgurudev Bhagwan) — Guru Maa Geeteshwari (Geeta Bhaskar)

"Jai Hanuman gyan gun sagar, jai kapis tihun lok ujagar."
Glory to Shri Hanuman! Your knowledge and virtues are fathomless. O Lord of the monkeys, your fame is in heaven, earth and the underworld. His life is deeper than the ocean; his virtues exceed the drops in the sea, and his knowledge is deeper than the ocean.

"Yug sahasra yojan par bhanu, leelyo tahi madhur phal janu."
In childhood Hanuman swallowed the sun, thinking it a fruit. The distance between sun and earth is 150.36 million km, confirmed by NASA. Hanuman did this about 1.5 crore years ago.

O Hanuman, may your grace fall like even a single drop into our lap, and we shall be blessed with supreme prasad.

Hanuman's many names: Ramdoot, Atulit Bal Dhama, Anjani-putra, Pavansut. He is Lord Ram's messenger and supreme servant. By Lord Ram's blessing he was granted immortality; the seven chiranjivis are Ashwatthama, Bali, Vyasa, Hanuman, Vibhishana, Kripa and Parashurama. Even today Hanuman is present in this Kali Yuga. Many have had his direct darshan. Goswami Tulsidas conversed with him many times. Our Sadgurudev Bhagwan also had his direct darshan. While worshipping Hanuman, he appeared before Shri Guru Maharaj ji. The 40-day anushthan started by Maharaj ji continues by his grace to this day: in Delhi, Faridabad, Malaysia and Bangkok, laddus are offered likewise.

Hanuman has Mother Sita's boon: "Ashta siddhi nau nidhi ke data, as bar din Janaki mata" — giver of the eight siddhis and nine treasures. Mother Sita blessed him that he would become the storehouse of these and distribute them to all devotees.

Hanuman has every kind of strength — bodily, intellectual, spiritual and strength in service.

Why "Anjaniputra"? When King Dasharatha, desiring children, performed the Putreshti yajna through Sage Shringi, Agni Dev emerged with a pot of payasam, which the king divided among the three queens. As they stood holding it, a kite swooped down, snatched a bowl of kheer from Queen Sumitra's hand and dropped it into Mother Anjani's lap — from which Hanuman was born. Lord Ram affirmed this, saying: "Raghupati kinhi bahut badai, tum mam priya Bharatahi sam bhai" — Hanuman, you are as dear to me as my brother Bharata.

Lord Ram's birth was on Navami; six days later Hanuman was born (as monkeys too are born in the ninth month). His physical birthday is celebrated a week after Ram Navami, and the second Hanuman Jayanti, the spiritual one, a day before Diwali.

Hanuman and the Sun-god: As son of Pavan, he flies like the wind. His guru of knowledge is Surya Narayan. He prayed: "I accept you as guru from my heart; please give me knowledge." Surya said he had no time to rest, so how could he sit and teach? Hanuman said he would fly alongside, then fly backward facing him, listening to his nectar-like words. Thus he learned all the sciences, except one — the science of householder life. Surya said Hanuman was a celibate and could not be taught it. Hanuman was dejected. Surya then said: "Do not worry; I will formally marry you to my daughter Suvarchala, and as soon as your learning is complete, both of you will go to separate places and perform austerities." Thus Surya did not leave Hanuman without that knowledge.

Why "Nama": When Lord Ram returned to Ayodhya, he honored everyone with gifts, but gave Hanuman nothing. Mother Sita removed her pearl necklace and gave it to Hanuman. He chewed each pearl and threw it away. Sita asked what he was doing. Hanuman humbly said: "Mother, if my Ram is not in these pearls, they are of no use to me." Someone in the assembly mocked: "Is Ram inside your huge body?" Hanuman tore open his chest and everyone saw Sita-Ram in his heart — and Ram's name on every hair of his body. From that day he was called "Nama."

Hanuman, steeped in Ram's name — may he shower grace on us and place that name, Ram, in our lap as remembrance.

Endless congratulations to all devotees on Hanuman Jayanti and crores of blessings of Shri Sadgurudev Bhagwan.

Page 18 — "Anadi-madhyantam-ananta-viryam…" — Rekha Bharadwaj

(Bhagavad Gita 11:19–20 explained)

Arjuna says: "This wondrous form of Yours fills all of space between heaven and earth and all directions; the three worlds tremble seeing this wondrous, terrible form, O Great Soul." (11:20)
"I see You without beginning, middle or end, with infinite power, infinite arms, sun and moon as Your eyes, Your mouth a blazing fire, heating the entire universe with Your radiance." (11:19)

Explanation: No particle exists where God is not. In the space between earth and sky, which seems empty, the Supreme is fully present. Krishna shows Arjuna that His incarnation is not limited to what he sees. For the gentle He is mild and peaceful; for the wicked His form is terrible. God has no change — no beginning, middle or end. The same hands that held the sweet flute also lifted Govardhan and slew demons (Putana, Shakatasura, Kamsa, Chanura, Trinavarta, Shishupala, Dantavakra). Seeing the terrible form, Arjuna becomes frightened and prays that Krishna withdraw it and show his gentle four-armed form. Krishna does so, and says that this form is extremely rare even for the gods and cannot be seen by chanting, austerity or sacrifice — only through single-minded devotion (ananya bhakti). In devotion alone, God keeps Himself in his devotee's control, forgetting even His own rules and honor.

Poem couplet (summarized): Gripped by intense love, I saw the Lord change His rules; His own honor may fall, but He never lets a devotee's honor fall.

Page 20 — Vibhuti Yoga — Shalini Vakta

The Bhagavad Gita is God's own word. For the welfare of humanity, God gave the Gita's knowledge through Arjuna, who had full faith and love for Him. God wants the entire world to have love and faith in Him, so from Chapter 7 He describes His glories (vibhutis). Knowing His glories in truth deepens devotion and love and turns us from the world toward God. In Chapters 7, 8 and 9 He describes His glories; Chapter 10 is especially "Vibhuti Yoga," in which He describes 72 vibhutis.

10:8 — "I am the origin of all; everything proceeds from Me; knowing this the wise worship Me with devotion." The whole world, animate and inanimate, arises from Him; He is the Supreme Father at the root of all creation. All creation, protection and dissolution happen through Him, as machines run by electricity.

10:10 — "To those constantly devoted, worshipping with love, I give the buddhi-yoga by which they come to Me." Devotees who firmly believe "we are God's and God is ours," and have no interest in worldly enjoyments, receive buddhi-yoga. Under favorable or unfavorable circumstances, praise or blame, their mind is undisturbed; they regard even buddhi-yoga as God's blessing.

Arjuna understands that knowing the vibhutis will deepen devotion, and asks in verse 18 that God describe them in detail. God agrees, but briefly, since His glories are endless. From verses 20 to 39 He gives 72 vibhutis. First: "I am the beginning, middle and end of beings."

10:29 — "Among the nagas I am Ananta (Shesha); among water-gods, Varuna; among ancestors, Aryama; among controllers, Yama."
- Shesha-naga is the king of snakes with a thousand hoods; he became God's bed in the ocean of milk and joined His leelas in incarnation.
- Varuna is lord of all water beings and water-gods and a devotee of God.
- Of the seven groups of ancestors (pitris), Aryama is chief. Param Pujya Guru Maa instructs to recite this verse at least 19 times during Pitru Paksha; she says the ancestors are pleased and bless us.
- Yamaraj purifies beings by making them bear the fruit of their deeds; his rule is just and righteous; he is God's devotee and a guardian of the worlds.

In this way God shows that all distinction, power and strength come from Him, not from this changing, perishable world. Believing this, our attraction goes to God, worldly thought falls away and contemplation of God arises.

Page 23 — The Importance of Shraddh

A good deed done with faith for one's ancestors is called Shraddh. It is also called Pitr-yajna. The 15 days of the dark fortnight of Ashwin are famous as Pitru Paksha. During these, people feed Brahmins and offer water for their ancestors and become free from ancestral debt. For those who died in any month or date, Shraddh is done on the same tithi in Pitru Paksha. For those who died on a full-moon day, Shraddh is prescribed on Bhadrapada Shukla Purnima.

"Shraddh" means whatever is given with faith ("shraddhaya diyate yat yat shraddham"). Shraddh satisfies ancestors for a year and they bless their children and family. By offering pinda, tarpan or shraddh to ancestors, a person gains long life, fame, honor and prosperity; there is no disease in the house and peace remains. Where shraddh is not done, some trouble remains, because the ancestors remain displeased.

Today some sects do not believe in shraddh and suffer for it. In Chapter 1 of the Gita, Arjuna was greatly concerned for his ancestors: if the family is destroyed, women become widows, mixed-caste children are born who cannot perform pinda-daan, tarpan and shraddh for the ancestors, who then fall (1:42).

Some doubt how food given to Brahmins can reach ancestors when we do not know in which birth they are now. But just as money sent by money order reaches a friend, so whatever form the ancestor is in, God converts the food into that form's nourishment and delivers it. And if they have attained God's abode, the merit still goes to the account of the one who performed it. God Himself said in the Gita, "Na me bhaktah pranashyati" — My devotee is never lost.

Childlessness today is also due to ancestral displeasure; those who do not perform shraddh do not have children in the next birth, so that none is left to perform their rites. Even Lord Ram performed shraddh and pinda-daan at Gaya for his father Dasharatha, who accepted it with his own hand, pleased by Ram's faith. Gaya is the sacred place; there is a boon that one who performs pinda-daan once there need not perform annual shraddh, though some donation on the tithi should still be given. For those troubled by ancestral wrath, the Gita has a remedy: the Maha-Pitr-yajna of the Gita destroys ancestral wrath, giving a childless person a son, relief from illness and prosperity.

Thus shraddh is very important. Do it with faith, be freed from ancestral debt and make life happy.

Pages 32–34 — Surdas's Krishna Bhakti, Love and Surrender — Niranjan Kumar Ranjan (Administrator, Geeta Dham)

Surdas's compositions are a unique example of Krishna devotion, in which the feeling of motherhood is felt — the outlook of a mother's affectionate heart and eyes seeing God, and the social values of the time, with priceless descriptions of Krishna's leelas.

Tulsidas saw Lord Ram as Maryada Purushottam; Meera saw Krishna as a lover; Surdas saw Krishna as a child, through motherhood. This is Vatsalya bhakti, which is why Surdas is called the "emperor of devotees." Though born blind, what he saw through the eyes of the mind is a unique and living description of worldly and otherworldly devotion.

Surdas was born in 1478 CE in the village of Runakta near Mathura. Born blind, he had the inner eyes of knowledge. Vallabhacharya initiated him into the Pushtimarg and told him: "Surdas ji, sing Krishna's leelas."

  • Vatsalya (parental love): The greatest feeling is child-leela. The joy Yashoda had, Sur put in words. In the Vedas and Gita Krishna is Yogeshwar; in Sur he is the child crawling on his knees. ("Yashoda Hari palne jhulavai…", "Maiya main nahin makhan khayo".) The false charge of butter-theft and Krishna's innocent denial are no philosophy, just a common child's feeling.
  • Sakhya (friendship): Krishna plays with cowherds, grazes cows, climbs the kadamba and plays pranks. Here all distinction between God and devotee ends.
  • Madhurya (sweet love): The gopis' "Bhramar-geet." When Uddhava comes with knowledge saying "Krishna is formless Brahman," the gopis reply: "Udho, man na bhaye dus bees…" — we have only one mind, and it went with Shyam. Sur says knowledge loses; love wins.

Harmony of Sur's devotion and the Gita: The Gita says "Yo mam pashyati sarvatra" (6:30) — one who sees Me everywhere. Surdas says he sees only his Kanhaiya everywhere, whether a kadamba tree or the Yamuna bank. The Gita says ananya bhakti; Sur says "Mero man anant kahan sukh pavai? Jaise udi jahaj ko panchhi, phir jahaj par avai" — like a bird flying from a ship that returns to the ship.

"Sur Sagar" has about 125,000 verses in 12 skandhas inspired by the Bhagavatam. Sur made Krishna human again — hungry, afraid of his mother, quarrelling with friends. Surdas's devotion is not with eyes closed but with eyes full of tears. He does not argue; he makes you weep as Yashoda.

Other pieces: virah (separation) after Krishna goes to Mathura; complete surrender (leaving ego); Krishna's human and divine nature together (mischievous child and Supreme Brahman).
Famous bhajans: "Hey Govind, hey Gopal, rakho sharan, ab to jeevan haare"; "Ankhiyan Hari darshan ki pyasi"; "Maiya mori main nahin makhan khayo"; "Prabhu ji mere avguna chit na dharo"; "Sabse unchi prem sagai."
For Surdas, born blind, Lord Krishna was never invisible but always present — that is Vatsalya bhakti, where devotee sings and God sits in the devotee's lap, delighted.

Page 52 — Amar Updesh (Immortal Teaching)

Shrimad Bhagavad Gita is the immortal teaching that came from the lotus mouth of the Lord. Any being, in whatever country or garb, who holds this teaching with faith leaves the state of mortality and attains nectar (immortality); there is no doubt in this.

The 12th chapter of the Gita is called the heart of God. Its unbroken recitation has been going on at Geeta Ashram, Mathura and Delhi Cantt for 50 years, and the Geeta family and Geeta lovers worldwide recite it with faith. Reading this chapter increases mental strength, willpower and faith in God.

"Mayyeva mana adhatsva mayi buddhim niveshaya; nivasishyasi mayyeva ata urdhvam na samshayah." (Gita 12:8) — Fix your mind on Me alone, place your intellect in Me; thereafter you will live in Me alone, without doubt.
